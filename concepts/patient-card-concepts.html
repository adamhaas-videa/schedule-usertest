Enamel · Schedule · Patient card

Six things, 280 × 228

Three concepts for the schedule patient card, each rendered in both conditional types — doctor restorative and hygiene visit. Every specimen below is built from Enamel semantic tokens at the card's existing footprint, so what you see is what fits.

Hard constraint. The card is 280 × 228 in the list, and duration-driven in the operatory grid — 88px at 30 min, 133px at 45, 178px at 60. Nothing here changes those numbers. The six needs have to be earned out of the existing middle region: 256 × ~79px, roughly five lines of type. Every concept is therefore an argument about what gets cut.
The six needs

Each specimen carries a coverage row: filled = surfaced on the card, outlined = surfaced but truncated, dashed = pushed to the drawer. The numbers are the ones from the brief, in the brief's order.

1Last-visit recapWhat was actually completed — prophy, x-rays, restorations.
2Outstanding treatment + whyRejected or deferred, in the patient's own words. Leland's first look.
3Discussed / recommended nextOpportunities raised last visit — SRP, night guard, crown.
4Recall statusNext cleaning booked or not. Hardest thing to find in the PMS today.
5Remaining benefitsChecked before any treatment conversation; shapes the framing.
6Health flagsAllergies, pre-med, chronic conditions.
Concept A

Flag Stack

The middle region becomes four ranked one-line flags — severity glyph, bold label, muted detail — plus a dimmed recap line at the floor. Rank order is the conditional logic: a restorative card leads with pre-med and money left on the table; a hygiene card leads with recall and perio. Nothing wraps, so the card is scannable at a glance down a column, and the cost is nuance — the patient's reasoning is clipped to a fragment.

Concept B

Pre / Post

One card, two states, switched by appointment status. Before the appointment it is a huddle brief: a written recap of the last visit with the patient's own words intact, and the open loops as chips. After, the same region flips to what was done today — and the red chips flip green as the loops close. Recall stops being a static field and becomes the thing the visit is measured by.

Conditional logic

What actually differs by type

One component, one rank function, two configurations. The card never branches on markup — it branches on which fields feed which slot, and in what priority.

Slot Doctor · restorative Hygiene visit
Rank 1 Health flag — pre-med, allergy, anticoagulant. Blocks the appointment if missed. Recall status — the hygiene visit's entire job is booking the next one.
Rank 2 Recall status — restorative patients fall out of prophy recall most often. Health flag — bleeding risk drives instrumentation choice.
Rank 3 Deferred restorative + reason — crown, MOD, endo. Quote verbatim. Perio status + SRP disposition — accepted / declined / never booked.
Rank 4 Discussed — night guard, whitening, ortho referral. Radiographs due — BWX interval, FMX interval.
Meta line Carrier · $ remaining · plan reset date. Reset date matters for “until January.” Carrier · $ remaining · preventive visits left. Counts, not just dollars.
Actions Voice note · Perio · Review. Post-visit: note + images. Perio first · Voice note · Review. Post-visit: note + images.
Never on card Full tx plan, ledger balance, claim history, chart notes. Full probing depths, recession map, home-care notes.
Where I'd land

Ledger for the grid, narrative for the huddle

Build first

C — Quote & Ledger

Fixed slots beat ranked ones once you are scanning eight columns. Recall lives in the same pixel on every card, which is the specific thing the PMS fails at today, and the quote gives Leland his first look without a click. It also degrades cleanly — the hero is always the hero at 88px.

Layer on

B — Pre / Post state

Not a competing card, a state machine on top of whichever body you pick. The post-visit state is worth building on its own merits: it turns the card into the front-desk handoff and makes “did we book the recall” answerable without opening anything.

Hold

A — Flag Stack

Best pure scan-ability of the three, but rank-ordering means the same field moves position card to card, so the eye has to read rather than land. Worth revisiting if the flag set turns out to be shorter and more stable than we think.

Open question for the data side. Three of the six — deferred-treatment reasoning, what was discussed, and recall intent — are not structured fields in most PMSs. They live in chart-note prose. Concepts B and C both assume something extracts them into short strings; A assumes it too, just less visibly. That extraction is the actual dependency here, not the layout.
