Banner · 28px
T1. Type, site, booked duration. Ground is tinted by track — it classifies the appointment, it does not alert about it. Present at every card size; the last element to degrade, not the first.
Identity
Name, start time, operatory, elapsed. No rule beneath it — the banner is already the division. Masked by privacy mode; nothing below depends on it.
Coverage chip
T6. Takes the status slot on upcoming and in-chair cards, because appointment status is already stated by the meta line and by the card's place on the grid — whereas coverage is stated nowhere else. Three states: Active, Not verified, Inactive. Hover gives the carrier plus remaining benefit or an unmet deductible; the full plan lives in the chart. On completed cards the slot reverts to the appointment status, since coverage is moot once the visit is billed.
Indicators
T3, reduced to glyphs. Allergy, pre-med, bleeding risk, systemic condition, perio stage. Severity comes from the tint, identity from the glyph — never colour alone. The card states that there is something to know; the chart states what. These never degrade: at 88px they shrink and move inline rather than dropping, because they are the only safety-critical thing on the card.
Plan
T2. Named This visit and stamped with a position — step 2 of 3, 3 steps, done — so the region can never be misread as history. Without that stamp a bare procedure line is ambiguous about tense, which is the single thing reviewers stumbled on. The banner uses the profession's shorthand (Crown seat, SRP) because it names the appointment; the plan uses plain verb phrases (fit and adjust, scale and root plane) because it describes actions and is read by front desk and stakeholders too. Hygiene carries a second provider inline — the doctor exam is a scheduled interrupt inside someone else's appointment.
Actions
Avatar, then voice note, perio, and Review. Order flips by track — perio leads on hygiene. No role label: the avatar's initials and colour carry the clinician, so DDS / RDH is redundant on a card whose banner already says which kind of visit it is.
Colour channels
Banner ground = appointment track. Card edge + avatar = clinician, from the muted palette in providerColors.ts. They coexist because they differ in weight, not just hue: the banner is a filled band, the edge is 1.5px. Initials carry the identity in text, so the colour is reinforcement rather than the message.
How the track is decided There is no separate visit-type field, and there should not be one. The track is a function of the procedure string — the app already does this in isHygieneProcedure(), matching prophylaxis, periodontal maintenance, scaling, root planing, srp, fluoride, sealants, deep cleaning, periodontal assessment. Everything else is the doctor track. One component, one derived flag, two content configurations — the markup never branches.
What Reviewed means Two rules that look like defects and are not. The flag is per-practice, not per-user — it answers whether anyone here has looked at this patient, which is why the card shows a bare Reviewed and never attributes it to a provider. And it does not reset when new imaging arrives. That would be stale state on any other surface, but recapture is reviewed in place: once a clinician is in imaging they read the new images during capture, and never come back out to the schedule expecting the badge to have changed. Store it as practice-scoped state on the appointment — not on the session, and not on the user.

My read: leave it off. The carry-over is the only element here that is never urgent — it changes how you talk to the patient, not what you do in the next ten minutes — and it is the one thing a clinician reads once, before the visit, rather than at a glance mid-chair. Conditional logic

What the track actually changes

Every region exists on both tracks. What differs is the vocabulary that fills it and the unit the site is measured in. Nothing here is a layout branch.

Region Doctor track Hygiene track
Banner · type Crown seat, crown prep, MOD composite, endo, extraction, implant placement. Verb + material. Prophy, perio maintenance, SRP, sealants, perio assessment. Interval + scope.
Banner · site Tooth number — #14, #19. Occasionally a surface: MOD, DO. Quadrant or arch — UR/UL, 4 quad, full mouth. Never a single tooth.
Indicators Weighted to what blocks or complicates the procedure: pre-med, allergy, anticoagulant. Same set, plus perio stage, which is the hygienist's running diagnosis rather than an alert.
Plan Sequential and irreversible. Order matters; you cannot cement before try-in. Sequential with a hand-off — the doctor exam interrupts mid-appointment and blocks dismissal.
Actions Voice note · Perio · Review → Reviewed. Post-visit: note, images, review state persists. Perio first · Voice note · Review → Reviewed. Same state, same persistence.
Never on card Full tx plan, ledger balance, claim history, chart notes, the patient's quote, the medication list behind a glyph. Full probing depths, recession map, home-care notes, historical prophy dates.

Two dependencies, not one. The banner is buildable today — procedure, duration and operatory are structured fields. The plan is not: Patient in mockPatients.ts carries a flat procedure string and a loose visitTags array, so site and plan[] still need naming. The indicators need less than the old gates did — allergies[] and conditionAlert already exist; what is missing is pre-med and anticoagulant flags.

Coverage is nearly there, with two gaps. Insurance in mockPatients.ts already carries carrier and remainingBenefit, so the chip and most of its tooltip are buildable today. But its status union is "Active" | "Pending" | "Inactive" while the three states here are Active / Not verified / Inactive — Pending wants renaming, because “pending” describes a claim in flight whereas “not verified” describes work the front desk still owes. And there is no deductible field anywhere in src/, so the unmet-deductible half of the tooltip has nothing behind it yet.

Two things to fix in providerColors.ts. First, the palette holds 8 entries and getProviderColor takes hash(id) % 8, so colours are not unique — in the current mock cast Beth Park and Dr. Elias Kovac both land on muted cyan. With the role label gone the avatar is doing more identification work than before, so the collision matters more than it did. Assign by index, or grow the palette past the provider count. Second, every entry is light-mode only. Deriving a dark tone by mixing these pastels toward the border collapses all eight onto the same grey — measured, two clinicians' edges came out 1.00:1 apart, which is not a subtle cue but an absent one. The specimens here carry an explicit dark tone per entry; the real palette needs the same pair.

What the edge can and cannot carry. At 1.5px the clinician tint is genuinely subtle — measured against the field it is 1.21:1 in light and 2.00:1 in dark, and two entries separate by an RGB distance of 15 and 27. That is enough to feel like a different card at a glance and not enough to name the clinician. The avatar is the identification; the edge is the reinforcement. The initials carry it in text, which is what keeps the cue from being colour-only.
